# Decupagem

Translate beats into shot duties before choosing files.

## Shot duties

- **Orient:** establish geography, time, participants, or process.
- **Reveal:** disclose information withheld by the prior shot.
- **Prove:** make a claim observable.
- **Contrast:** oppose scale, emotion, direction, texture, or meaning.
- **Intensify:** increase proximity, movement, density, or stakes.
- **Humanize:** show effort, vulnerability, reaction, or relationship.
- **Transition:** bridge spaces, clients, phases, or visual ideas.

## Coverage grammar

Build sequences from complementary functions:

1. geography or scale;
2. medium action;
3. close human expression;
4. tactile or operational detail;
5. reaction or consequence;
6. exit, reset, or transition.

Do not require all six. Avoid consecutive shots with the same subject, scale, motion, and information.

## Shot-plan fields

Record:

- beat and shot duty;
- source project/client;
- subject and action;
- shot size and angle;
- camera and subject movement;
- foreground, midground, and background;
- emotional intent;
- ideal and acceptable duration;
- source time range;
- audio relevance;
- transition logic;
- rejection conditions.

## Footage selection

Prefer:

- complete and intentional camera movement;
- readable action with a useful entry and exit;
- stable focus and exposure;
- layered composition and clean subject separation;
- authentic reaction or consequence;
- visual compatibility with adjacent shots;
- project/client alternation when range is part of the claim.

Reject:

- movement errors, searching focus, accidental reframing, or dead tails;
- embedded watermarks, finished-client graphics, or duplicate brand exposure;
- shots that depend on context unavailable in the edit;
- redundant coverage without a distinct narrative duty.

## Continuity choices

Maintain or intentionally break:

- screen direction;
- eyeline;
- motion phase;
- action match;
- spatial geography;
- luminance and color temperature;
- sound perspective;
- emotional intensity.

State the reason for a discontinuity. Use motivated mismatches for contrast, not by accident.
