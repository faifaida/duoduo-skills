# Storytelling

## Strategic premise

Write: “For [audience in initial state], this film makes [new belief or feeling] credible so they [desired action], supporting [business outcome].”

Separate:

- communication outcome: what viewers understand, feel, remember, or desire;
- behavioral outcome: what viewers do next;
- business outcome: what the organization can measure.

## Default case spine

1. **Desired result:** what needed to happen beyond the screen.
2. **Tension:** the human, commercial, or institutional obstacle.
3. **Audiovisual idea:** the organizing creative answer.
4. **Execution:** the decisions that made the idea tangible.
5. **Transformation:** the change experienced by people or the organization.
6. **Proof:** verified evidence, not inferred success.
7. **Signature:** the producer's point of view and next action.

## Beat map

For each beat record:

- information introduced;
- emotion before and after;
- unanswered question;
- visual duty;
- audio duty;
- proof required;
- approximate duration.

A beat must change knowledge, feeling, expectation, or direction. Merge adjacent beats that do the same work.

## Story engines

- **Contrast:** expected state against revealed reality.
- **Escalation:** increasing scale, stakes, intimacy, or tempo.
- **Discovery:** withhold context and let details assemble meaning.
- **Transformation:** show a legible before/after in behavior or perception.
- **Accumulation:** repeated evidence becomes the argument.
- **Countdown:** a deadline or opening creates forward pressure.

Choose one dominant engine. Use another only for a deliberate act change.

## Designed surprise

Create surprise by breaking an established rule:

- rapid montage → long human hold;
- music peak → silence;
- polished scale → intimate imperfection;
- familiar claim → challenging lettering;
- expected hero → unseen contributor;
- chronological setup → consequence first.

Plant the rule before breaking it. Surprise without setup is randomness.
