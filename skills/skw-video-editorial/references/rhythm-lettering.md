# Rhythm and lettering

## Rhythm

Design rhythm as changing density, not a constant cut rate.

Useful patterns:

- **Pulse:** repeat a duration family, then shorten it.
- **Wave:** alternate dense and breathable passages.
- **Escalation:** shorten shots while increasing scale or consequence.
- **Hold:** stay after the expected cut to create intimacy or tension.
- **Silent hammer:** establish sound and motion, cut both, then land the claim.
- **False ending:** resolve, leave a gap, then reveal the stronger consequence.

For a high-variety hero opening, test:

`0.5 s × 3 → 0.3 s × 4 → 0.2 s × 5 → interruption → 1.5–2.5 s hold`

Treat this as a starting hypothesis. Align cuts to meaningful motion, phrase accents, and comprehension.

## Lettering

Lettering may:

- issue a challenge;
- create tension with the image;
- complete a thought across cuts;
- name the consequence;
- turn evidence into a proposition;
- deliver the CTA.

Avoid literal descriptions of what is already visible.

Build lines as a progression:

1. destabilize the expected claim;
2. establish the criterion;
3. show or name consequence;
4. invite the next action.

Example:

```text
Bonito não basta.
O vídeo precisa fazer alguma coisa acontecer.
Desejo. Confiança. Movimento.
Qual resultado precisa acontecer agora?
```

Keep each line readable at the actual display size. Let decisive words occupy their own beat when emphasis matters.
