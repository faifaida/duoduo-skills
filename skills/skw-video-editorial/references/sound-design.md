# Sound design

Treat audio as an argument with its own setup, development, interruption, and payoff.

## Layers

- **Music:** macro energy and emotional contract.
- **Production sound:** credibility, place, tactility, and human presence.
- **Designed SFX:** emphasis, transition, scale, or expectation.
- **Voice:** meaning, testimony, interpretation, or provocation.
- **Silence:** separation, vulnerability, reset, or impact.

## Audio-first map

Before fine cutting:

1. mark musical phrases, downbeats, breaks, and climax windows;
2. select production sounds that can lead or bridge scenes;
3. place the main interruption and define what disappears;
4. assign sound perspective to close, medium, and wide imagery;
5. reserve headroom for lettering impacts or voice.

Use J-cuts to create anticipation and L-cuts to preserve consequence. Do not add a whoosh to every visual transition.

## Designed surprise

Possible structures:

- accelerating impacts → hard vacuum → dry human sound;
- polished music → imperfect production sound → new groove;
- familiar corporate cadence → off-axis sonic texture;
- apparent climax → silence → understated proof.

The interruption must support the proposition. If it only calls attention to editing, simplify it.

## Selection and mix checks

- production sound is synchronized and meaningful;
- SFX varies by material, distance, and scale;
- music does not flatten every beat into the same emotion;
- voice remains intelligible without over-ducking;
- silence is intentional and survives delivery loudness;
- no unlicensed source is assumed safe for publication.
